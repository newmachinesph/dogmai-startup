Legal

Written to be read, not survived.

Our policies are drafted for the people who actually use the platform. Where law requires precision, we're precise; everywhere else, we're plain.

Privacy, in principle

What we collect: account details for practitioners (including identity verification for Attest), practice content practitioners upload to train their own voice models, and — only with explicit, revocable seeker consent — the longitudinal context seekers choose to carry between sessions.

What we never do: sell personal data; train on client readings or seeker memory; pool one practitioner's voice model with another's; retain a voice model after its owner departs. Deletion requests are honored in full, attestation records excepted only where the seeker who holds the reading chooses to keep its seal verifiable.

Where data lives: encrypted at rest and in transit, hosted in the US and EU, with regional residency options for Sanctum tiers.

Seeker rights: seekers can view, export, or erase their memory at any time, without a support ticket, regardless of which practitioner's practice it sits in.

Terms, in principle

The service: DOGM.AI provides software and payment infrastructure to spiritual practitioners. We are not a party to the practitioner–seeker relationship, we do not provide readings, and nothing on the platform constitutes medical, legal, financial, or psychological advice.

Guardrails are conditions of use: the refusal categories (health prognoses, death predictions, pregnancy determinations, specific financial picks), disclosure defaults, and crisis protocols may not be disabled or circumvented. Attempts to do so are grounds for termination — this is the covenant that keeps the platform viable for everyone.

Truthful claims: practitioners agree not to misrepresent AI involvement in their work. Attest exists so that honesty is the easy default.

Your exit: leave any time. Your client list, content archive, voice model export, and attestation records go with you; our copies of your voice model are deleted. Ninety days of transition support on request.

Entertainment-services framing: where jurisdictions require spiritual services be offered “for entertainment purposes,” practitioner storefronts include the applicable language automatically — one more thing you shouldn't have to become a lawyer to get right.
